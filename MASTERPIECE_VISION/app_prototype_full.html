The Thinking Gym · one worthy problem
Sit with it.
★★★☆ Challenge Number Theory
Find the smallest positive integer n such that n! ends in exactly 10 zeros.
🎉 Yes — 45! ends in exactly 10 zeros. Beautifully done.
Nudge Trailing zeros come from factors of 10 — each is a 2 paired with a 5. Which is rarer in a factorial?
Strategy So zeros = number of 5s = ⌊n/5⌋ + ⌊n/25⌋ + … Hunt the smallest n where that hits 10.
A worked step n=45: ⌊45/5⌋=9, ⌊45/25⌋=1 → 10. And 44 gives only 9. You've got it.
The soul of Olympiad · many beautiful paths
Three ways to see it.
Elementary · clearestZeros come from 5s (2s are plentiful). Count multiples of 5, add one more per 25, 125… For 10: 45 → 9+1=10; 44 → 9. n=45.
Slick · LegendreThe exponent of prime p in n! is (n−s)/(p−1) with s the base-p digit sum. p=5 lands on 45 in one move.
💬 By Vihaan, L4"I listed where each new zero appears — 25 adds two at once, so the count skips 5. There's no n with exactly 5 zeros. Cool!" — 41 ▲
