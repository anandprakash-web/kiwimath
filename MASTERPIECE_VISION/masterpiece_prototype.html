Pick any 3-digit number. Reverse it, subtract the smaller from the larger. Do it again with the answer's reverse… you always land on 1089.
Why on earth would that be true? Take a guess — then let's find out.
Why it's beautiful It's place value in disguise: the subtraction is always a multiple of 99, and the second step forces the middle to 9 and the ends to sum to 9. The "trick" is real number theory wearing a cape. 🦸

★★★☆ Challenge Number Theory
Find the smallest positive integer n such that n! ends in exactly 10 zeros.
🎉 Yes — 45! ends in exactly 10 zeros. Beautifully done.
Nudge Zeros at the end of a number come from factors of 10 — and every 10 is a 2 paired with a 5. Which is rarer in a factorial, 2s or 5s?
Strategy So the number of trailing zeros = the number of 5s in n!. Count them: ⌊n/5⌋ + ⌊n/25⌋ + … Now you're hunting the smallest n where that count hits 10.
A worked step Try n = 45: ⌊45/5⌋ = 9, ⌊45/25⌋ = 1 → 9 + 1 = 10. And 44 gives only 9. So the smallest n is… (you've got it).

The soul of Olympiad · many beautiful paths
Three ways to see it.
Elementary · clearest Trailing zeros come from factors of 5 (2s are plentiful). Count multiples of 5 up to n, then add an extra for each 25, 125… For 10 zeros: 45 gives 9 + 1 = 10; 44 gives only 9. n = 45.
Slick · Legendre's formula The exponent of a prime p in n! is (n − s)/(p−1), where s is the digit-sum of n in base p. Setting p = 5 and solving for 10 lands you on the same 45 — fewer steps, more machinery.
💬 Community · by Vihaan, L4 "I just listed where a new zero appears: 5,10,15,20 (×1 each)… 25 adds two! So zeros go 1,2,3,4,6,7,8,9,10 at 5,10,15,20,25,30,35,40,45. Notice it skips 5 zeros — there's no n with exactly 5! Cool, right?" — 41 ▲
